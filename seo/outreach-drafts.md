# Link outreach drafts

Three emails, ready to send. They pitch a guide, never the shop, and they ask
for nothing in exchange. That is deliberate: every other link channel in
`seo/backlinks.json` is blocked until there are products to sell, and the moment
one of these emails mentions a supplier it joins them.

Expected yield is low single figures per hundred. Send them in batches, keep the
ones that reply, and do not follow up more than once.

Before sending any of these, open the target page and check it actually has a
resources or useful-links section. An email asking a page to add a link it has
no place for is the commonest reason this fails.

---

## 1. Councils and state health, for sharps disposal

The likeliest first win. Councils publish a sharps page, those pages are usually
thin, and the person who maintains it is reachable.

**Subject:** Sharps disposal explainer, if it is useful for your page

> Hello,
>
> I noticed your page on sharps disposal while looking at how councils explain
> it around the country.
>
> We have written a plain explainer on what actually goes in a sharps container,
> what does not, and how to find a drop-off point. It is written for people
> doing this at home for the first time, which seemed to be the gap on most of
> the pages I read.
>
> https://goodpracticesupply.com.au/learn/sharps-disposal-australia
>
> No obligation at all. If it is useful for your resources list, please use it.
> If not, no reply needed.
>
> Thanks,
> Josh Peacock
> Good Practice Supply

## 2. Nursing and paramedicine educators, for needle gauge

Gauge running backwards is the thing every cohort asks about. A lecturer who
links a good explainer tends to keep it linked for years.

**Subject:** Needle gauge explainer for your students

> Hello,
>
> I write reference material on injection consumables, and the question that
> comes up more than any other is why the gauge numbers run backwards.
>
> We have put together a page that explains it without selling anything: what
> the number means, why 31G is finer than 29G, how the ISO hub colours work, and
> where length matters more than gauge.
>
> https://goodpracticesupply.com.au/learn/needle-numbers-explained
>
> If it is useful for a subject guide or a reading list, you are very welcome to
> it. If not, no trouble.
>
> Thanks,
> Josh Peacock

## What not to do

- Do not pitch the shop or ask to be listed as a supplier. Those asks belong to
  the other four channels and all four are blocked until there is a catalogue to
  stand behind.
- Do not offer anything in exchange for a link.
- Do not send a second follow-up.
- Do not send to a page that has nowhere to put a link.
